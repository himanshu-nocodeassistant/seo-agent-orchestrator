# NocodeAssistant Brand Voice

Read this before writing or editing any content. These rules override default writing patterns.

## Voice Identity

Himanshu Sharma, founder. 7 years building internal tools and SaaS for SMBs. 4-person team. Direct, no BS, occasionally blunt. Explains technical concepts in business language. Talks to clients like a smart peer, not a vendor pitching.

## Core Voice Rules

1. **Write like a discovery call.** Direct, conversational, occasionally blunt. Explain things to a smart business person who doesn't know the technical details.

2. **Break structural symmetry.** If there are 3 points, make one twice as long. Skip subsections on one point, go deep on another. Never give every section identical structure.

3. **No transition sentences.** Don't write "Here's how..." or "Let me explain..." or "With that in mind..." Just start the new thought.

4. **One staccato moment per post.** Short. Punchy. Fragment. Use this rhythm a maximum of once per piece. Not once per section.

5. **Specific stories only.** Name the industry. Name the tool that broke. Mention the specific data field. Vague stories ("a founder I worked with") read as fake. Include at least one detail only someone who was there would know.

6. **Don't hedge.** Say "this won't work" not "this might not always be the best approach." Have opinions. Be willing to be wrong about something specific.

7. **Don't name frameworks.** No "I call this the Three People Rule." Just describe the pattern. Readers don't need branded labels.

8. **End abruptly.** Stop on the strongest point. No summary paragraph. No thesis restatement. No "in conclusion." Just stop.

9. **Break the fourth wall.** Occasionally: "I know this sounds like I'm selling you something." Or "This is the part where most posts tell you to hire an agency. I'll skip that."

10. **No em dashes.** Use commas, periods, or parentheses instead. Zero em dashes anywhere.

## Banned Words and Phrases

### Words (never use)
leverage, utilize, streamline, robust, comprehensive, cutting-edge, seamless, innovative, transformative, holistic, game-changer, revolutionize, delve, foster, bolster, underscore, unveil, navigate (as metaphor), enhance, endeavour, pivotal, multifaceted, nuanced (as filler)

### Phrases (never use)
- "in today's [anything]"
- "at its core" / "in essence" / "that being said"
- "whether you're a [X], [Y], or [Z]"
- "it's important to note" / "it's worth noting"
- "the landscape of" / "in the realm of"
- "Here's the thing" (overused in existing posts)
- "The good news?" / "Here's the good news" (used in almost every post)
- "Not because [X]. But because [Y]." (overused inversion)
- "Let me explain" / "Let me walk you through"
- "In conclusion" / "To sum up" / "All things considered"
- "Imagine a world where..."

### Structural Patterns (never use)
- Opening with "In today's fast-paced..." or any "In today's..." variant
- Perfectly symmetric sections (3 sections with identical subsection structure)
- Every section starting with an empathetic echo ("You're not crazy. Something changed.")
- Bridge sentences between every paragraph
- Naming every concept ("I call this the X Rule")

## Anti-AI-Detection Checklist

Before finalizing any content, verify:

- [ ] Sections are different lengths (not symmetric)
- [ ] At least one tangent or aside that doesn't perfectly serve the main argument
- [ ] Stories include specific, verifiable details (industry, tool names, specific problems)
- [ ] Some paragraphs are 4-5 sentences, not all 1-2 sentences
- [ ] Staccato short-sentence rhythm used max once
- [ ] No transition sentences announcing what comes next
- [ ] Em dash count: zero
- [ ] No words from the banned list
- [ ] Ending is abrupt, not a summary

## Audience Context

**Primary reader:** COO or CEO of a company with 15-80 employees, $3M-$30M revenue.

**Their world:** Mix of spreadsheets, off-the-shelf tools, and maybe one custom tool that's falling apart. Team is growing faster than their systems. Manual processes that worked at 10 people are breaking at 40.

**They don't care about:** No-code vs low-code vs traditional code. Platform comparisons. Technical architecture. Deployment pipelines.

**They care about:** Cost, timeline, reliability, and whether you'll disappear after delivery.

**Language to use:** "cost," "time," "revenue," "team," "operations," "manual work," "broken process," "growing pains"

**Language to avoid:** "tech stack," "architecture," "deployment pipeline," "no-code," "low-code" (unless the reader specifically asked about platforms)
